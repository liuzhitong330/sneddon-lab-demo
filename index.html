Motivation Endocrine trajectory Lineage tracing Relevance

The Sneddon Lab studies how pancreatic endocrine cells acquire identity—and how that developmental logic can improve the generation and transplantation of functional replacement beta cells. A key transition sits between an NGN3-positive progenitor and a hormone-producing cell: a FEV-expressing interval in which alpha and beta trajectories begin to separate.

I built an interactive evidence map of that interval. It connects the lab's embryonic single-cell timecourse to pseudotime, in vivo lineage tracing, and branch-associated markers so that each reported result can be inspected in its developmental context.

How it works

The first analysis follows four states reconstructed from the E14.5 endocrine compartment and places them against the public GSE101099 timecourse. The second compares Fev-Cre lineage labeling across five endocrine outcomes, then tests the asymmetry of two markers validated along alpha- and beta-directed branches.

This is a structured reading of reported measurements—not a new computation on the expression matrix. Every number below comes from Byrnes et al. (2018) or the linked GEO record.

Alpha, beta, and delta cells were fully labeled, and gamma cells were nearly fully labeled. Epsilon cells were different: only 23.2% were traced. The evidence therefore supports FEV as a major endocrine route without claiming it is obligatory for every lineage.

Why this matters for the Sneddon Lab

A state-resolved FEV window creates an experimentally useful bridge between developmental mapping and the lab's translational work. In human pluripotent stem-cell differentiation, FEV, GNG12, and PEG10 could be followed across a dense timecourse, then linked to later glucose-responsive insulin secretion and transplant performance.

The honest next question is predictive rather than descriptive: do cells that enter a FEV+/GNG12+ program reliably become functional beta cells across hPSC lines, culture batches, and transplant contexts?

Methods note

This demo organizes paper-reported cell counts, marker expression, lineage-tracing percentages, and inferred developmental relationships from Byrnes et al. It is not a raw-data reanalysis and does not establish that GNG12 or PEG10 causes lineage choice. The core trajectory comes from murine embryonic pancreas; its relevance to human fetal tissue and hESC differentiation was supported in the paper, but functional prediction and transplantation outcomes remain open experimental questions.
