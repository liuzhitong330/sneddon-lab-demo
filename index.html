When does pancreatic endocrine fate become visible?

A paper-grounded map places a Fev-expressing state between Ngn3-positive progenitors and hormone-producing cells, then asks which signals begin to distinguish alpha from beta fate.

By Cathy Liu · prepared for the Sneddon Lab, UCSF

The thesis

The developmental sequence is more resolved than a binary progenitor-to-hormone transition. Sneddon Lab single-cell RNA-seq, pseudotime, and lineage tracing support an intermediate Fev-expressing state: Fev turns on after Ngn3, before hormone acquisition, and is traversed by most mature endocrine lineages. Within that interval, Gng12 and Peg10 provide experimentally validated signals of beta- and alpha-directed trajectories.

Intermediate state

Fev-high cells express Pax4, Chga/b, and Neurod1, but not mature Ins1 or Gcg and not the earlier marker Ngn3.

Pseudotime placed Fev+/Pax4+ and Fev-high/Chgb+ cells between Ngn3+ progenitors and alpha or beta cells. In lineage-traced E14.5 pancreas, about 20% of Ngn3-lineage cells were Fev-high.

03 · Lineage tracing

Most endocrine lineages pass through Fev

Choose an endocrine lineage. Each percentage is the fraction of hormone-positive E14.5 cells labeled by Fev-Cre lineage tracing.

Interpretation. The contrast is informative: alpha, beta, and delta cells were fully labeled, gamma cells were nearly fully labeled, while epsilon cells were only partially labeled. Fev therefore marks a major endocrine route, but not an obligatory route for every embryonic endocrine lineage.

04 · A branch begins to resolve

Gng12 and Peg10 separate beta- and alpha-directed evidence

The paper validated pseudotime-associated branch markers with in situ hybridization. Switch between the proposed outcomes to compare the asymmetry.

95.8% of beta cells expressed Gng12
30.5% of beta cells expressed Peg10

Gng12 was already detectable in the Fev-high interval and became strongly enriched in beta cells, supporting it as a candidate signal of beta-directed allocation after Fev onset.

05 · A next experiment

Test whether branch signals predict functional beta-cell maturity

A useful extension would connect developmental state assignment to the lab's stem-cell and transplantation workflow. During hPSC differentiation, one could sort or index cells across the Ngn3-to-FEV interval, then ask whether early GNG12-high and PEG10-high programs predict later glucose-responsive insulin secretion after maturation.

  1. Measure FEV, GNG12, PEG10, and endocrine markers across a dense hPSC differentiation timecourse using flow cytometry and single-cell RNA-seq.
  2. Link each early state to mature identity through lineage barcoding or compatible temporal tracing.
  3. Validate beta-cell function in vitro and after transplantation, while testing whether the branch signal generalizes across hPSC lines.

This bridges the Sneddon Lab's developmental, genomic, tissue-engineering, and transplantation approaches. It also fits my experience in mammalian cell culture, flow cytometry, mouse models, and single-cell analysis in R and Python.

Methods and limits

A structured reading of reported evidence

This demo organizes paper-reported cell counts, lineage-tracing percentages, marker validation, and inferred developmental relationships from Byrnes et al. (2018). It is not a reanalysis of the 496.6 MB processed expression matrix, and it does not infer unreported effect sizes or causal mechanisms. The trajectory is a preclinical developmental model; translation to human stem-cell differentiation and functional transplantation remains an experimental question.
